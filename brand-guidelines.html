Who We Are

Monroe Elementary is a joyful, multilingual public school in San Francisco's Excelsior neighborhood, committed to community, equity, and belonging for every family.

Mission

We serve a diverse community of families — in English, Spanish, and Cantonese — and are committed to building a school where every child belongs, every family is welcomed, and the community takes care of each other.

Core Values

  • Community over institution — we center families, not bureaucracy
  • Liberation and equity — anti-racist, culturally affirming, justice-minded
  • Radical welcome — every family deserves access, information, and belonging
  • Social-emotional learning — the whole child matters
  • Multilingual identity — English, Spanish, and Cantonese are equally honored
  • Mutual aid — we look out for each other

Our Audiences

Current Monroe Families Students Prospective Families Community Partners Volunteers SFUSD Staff

Voice & Tone

Our voice is the personality behind everything we write and say. It stays consistent. Our tone shifts with context — warmer in community messages, clearer in resource documents.

"We take care of each other."

Monroe's north star — everything we write should feel like this.

Voice Principles

Warm and Direct

We speak like a trusted neighbor, not a government office. We say "We've got you" not "Please be advised."

Community-First

Language centers the reader: "You can find..." not "Parents are encouraged to..." We avoid institutional distance.

Hopeful and Real

We acknowledge difficulty honestly while holding space for possibility. No empty positivity — but always pointing toward what's possible.

Multilingual by Default

All primary communications are available in English, Spanish, and Cantonese. Translation is never an afterthought.

Inclusive and Liberatory

Gender-neutral language. No default assumptions of ability, housing status, or immigration status. We reflect the full humanity of our community.

Tone by Context

ContextToneExample
Homepage / heroConfident, welcoming, energetic"We take care of each other."
Community HubWarm, practical, non-judgmental"Need something? We've got you."
Event announcementsFriendly, clear, action-oriented"Join us Thursday — food provided."
Resource documentsClear, accessible, plain language"To apply, bring two forms of ID."
FundraisingGratitude-first, community-minded"Every dollar stays at Monroe."
Emergency / urgentCalm, clear, direct"School is closed tomorrow, May 20."

Voice Do's and Don'ts

DO
✓Use plain language families can act on
✓Center the reader: "You can..."
✓Use "caregivers" or "families" (inclusive)
✓Acknowledge difficulty honestly
✓Translate all primary content upfront
DON'T
✗Use bureaucratic or legalistic language
✗Center the institution: "Monroe provides..."
✗Default to "parents" (excludes guardians)
✗Paper over real challenges with positivity
✗Add "Translation available upon request"

Design Elements

Monroe's visual language is warm, handcrafted, and energetic. These recurring elements create cohesion across all pages.

Flat Box Shadows

Buttons and key UI elements use flat, offset solid shadows — no blur, no gradient. Tactile, print-like quality.

Brush Stroke Underlines

Key words in hero headings get SVG brush stroke underlines using feTurbulence filters. Organic, hand-painted feel. Always Monarch Orange, full opacity.

We take care of each other.

Rainbow Stripe

Horizontal stripe of all brand colors — decorative accent, typically at section tops or as a footer divider. Color order: Poppy · Monarch · Marigold · Jade · Cyan · Plum · Magenta.

Ink Borders & Structure

  • Standard border: 1.5px solid #1A1410 (full ink)
  • Subtle dividers: rgba(26,20,16, 0.12–0.20)
  • Dashed: used for internal footer dividers only
  • Never use border-radius on primary UI boxes (cards, panels, buttons use pill-radius or none)

Decorative Motifs

  • Butterflies — black silhouette, tinted to brand colors via CSS filter; used as small accent illustrations
  • Chinese lattice — subtle background texture at 0.04 opacity
  • Half-circles — geometric decorative accents at section edges
  • Always secondary — never let motifs compete with content

Community Hub

The Community Hub is Monroe's most distinctive feature — a multilingual, community-centered resource portal with its own visual sub-identity.

"Need something? We've got you.
Want to help? We'll find a match."

The Hub's voice — held, practical, never judgmental.

Five Sections

i.Get Connected
ii.Community Care
iii.Resources
iv.Volunteer
v.Lunch Menu

Hub Values

  • Free — all services and resources at no cost
  • Confidential — information shared stays within the community
  • Multilingual — fully available in English, Spanish, and Cantonese
  • Non-judgmental — every family's situation is valid
  • Community-built — driven by what Monroe families actually need

Hub Voice Rules

  • Language is trauma-informed and non-stigmatizing
  • Instructions are clear and step-by-step — families in stress need low cognitive load
  • Say "We'll find a match" not "Volunteer opportunities are available"
  • Speak more softly than the main site — held, not energetic

Multilingual Standards

Language access is a right, not a courtesy. These standards ensure we honor that across all communications.

English Español 中文
  • All primary web pages have content available in all three languages via Google Translate
  • High-stakes content (enrollment, Hub resources, emergency notices) must be human-translated
  • Language switcher always visible in the utility bar at the top of every page
  • Language labels use each language's own script: English · Español · 中文
  • Chinese label uses ZCOOL XiaoWei font and Magenta color in the utility bar
  • Spanish label uses Marigold color in the utility bar
  • Design for text expansion — Spanish and Chinese text can be 20–40% longer than English

Multilingual Do's and Don'ts

DO
✓Translate all primary content before publishing
✓Use human translation for Hub and enrollment content
✓Display language labels in their own scripts
✓Design flexible layouts that handle text expansion
DON'T
✗Launch in English only and add translations later
✗Rely on machine translation for high-stakes content
✗Write "Chinese" instead of "中文"
✗Design tight layouts that break in other languages

Page Structure

  • Every page: Utility Bar → Masthead/Nav → Content → Footer
  • Utility bar: address, phone, ParentSquare button, language switcher
  • Masthead: logo (200px), centered nav, Tour + Give buttons
  • Mobile nav: hamburger → full-screen overlay

Navigation Rules

  • Active page: bottom border in Cyan on the nav link
  • Hover: color shifts to Monarch Orange (no underline on nav)
  • Dropdowns: 260px min-width, 1.5px solid Ink border, 5px flat box-shadow
  • Home is always the first nav item

Accessibility

  • All images: alt text or aria-hidden="true" for decorative
  • Color is never the sole means of conveying information
  • Minimum contrast: 4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text
  • All interactive elements have visible focus states

Performance

  • Non-hero images: loading="lazy"
  • Google Translate script: async
  • Google Fonts: preconnect + display=swap
  • Grain overlay: single fixed-position SVG via grain.js — never duplicated

Imagery Guidelines

Authentic community photography over stock. Warm, diverse, candid. Every image should feel like Monroe.

Photography

  • Authentic community photography preferred over stock images
  • Images reflect the full diversity of Monroe families
  • Warm, natural lighting — avoid cold or clinical looks
  • Candid moments over posed — community energy over performance
  • Student images require proper media release consent

Illustrations & Icons

  • Butterfly motifs: black silhouette, tinted to brand colors via CSS filter
  • Cultural patterns (Chinese lattice) used as subtle texture only
  • Icons: thin-stroke, consistent weight — avoid filled or cartoonish styles
  • All decorative images: aria-hidden="true"

Imagery Do's and Don'ts

DO
✓Use authentic photos of Monroe students and families
✓Show diversity across race, family structure, ability
✓Use warm, community-centered images
✓Scale decorative motifs to complement content
DON'T
✗Use generic stock photography of children
✗Default to imagery centering white, nuclear families
✗Use clinical, institutional, or test-focused imagery
✗Let decorative elements overpower content
